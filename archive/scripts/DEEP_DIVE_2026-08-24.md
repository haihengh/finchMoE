# Deep Dive: Localization of the finchMoE generation defect (2026-08-24, updated 2026-08-27)

Status: the per-step mechanism is fully identified and quantitatively verified,
and the causal chain is now CLOSED by a counterfactual. The remaining work is a
scoped kernel-ordering fix, not further diagnosis.

## Headline

finchMoE's broken-code generations (missing newlines, 1-space indents) are NOT
caused by a layer formula bug, a weight bug, or a sampling bug. They are caused
by a **small hidden-state precision accumulation** whose specific direction
suppresses newline/indent token logits by 0.4-1.0 through the shared lm_head,
flipping greedy whitespace decisions. The counterfactual (identical math in
BLAS order) scores 100% on the slice: the accumulation is seeded purely by
**Metal matmul reduction ordering**, not by the formulation.

## The evidence chain

### 1. finchMoE matches llama.cpp's exact recurrence per layer

Per-layer residual streams at position 152, llama.cpp in per-token
(autoregressive) mode vs finchMoE (`FINCHMOE_LAYER_DUMP`):

| layer | maxd | corr |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.002 | 0.9997 |
| 15 | 0.050 | 0.9898 |
| 30 | 0.155 | 0.9863 |
| 38 | 0.262 | 0.9925 |

The divergence grows slowly (0.0003 -> 0.034 rmsd pre-norm) - a precision
accumulation across all 40 layers, not a single-layer bug. The earlier
"maxd 42 at layer 10" result was llama.cpp's CHUNKED prefill diverging from
its own per-token path, NOT finchMoE diverging (verified by comparing llama
chunked vs llama per-token).

### 2. The final-logit difference is 100% explained by the hidden difference

At the lm_head (Q6_K dequantized with a fixed Python reference, verified
against llama.cpp's own result_output at corr 0.99996):

```
logit_diff = output.weight * (norm(h_finchmoe) - norm(h_llama))
```

- correlation(predicted, measured) over 2000+ tokens: **0.999**
- predicted rms 0.3882 == measured rms 0.3882 (exact)
- newline tokens, predicted vs measured: '\n' -0.415/-0.407, '\n\n'
  -0.755/-0.793, '    \n' -0.640/-0.641, ' ' -1.028/-1.034

There is NO separate per-token-class bias. The newline/indent tokens are the
lm_head rows most sensitive to the accumulated hidden difference.

### 3. Why newline tokens (not just high-logit tokens)

Rejected hypothesis: logit diff proportional to logit magnitude (a norm/scale
effect). corr(diff, logit) = -0.06, slope -0.011, top-500 tokens shift ~0,
bottom-500 shift +0.17. The suppression is specific to the newline-row
subspace, not to high logits in general.

### 4. Greedy amplification

At near-tie whitespace decision points the -0.4..-1.0 shifts flip the argmax
toward newline-free tokens (e.g. BPE id 91877 `:result`, ` sorted_numbers`
with a single leading space). Greedy (temp 0, top-k 1, rep-penalty disabled)
locks in the broken structure.

### 5. The docstring loop is a separate attractor

Fixed independently by `--rep-penalty 1.05` (0/20 slice tasks loop; the
engine's built-in default is 1.15 - the eval disabled it with 1.0).

## What was ruled out

- Embedding lookup: bit-exact vs the file (corr 1.0).
- lm_head dequant: finchMoE's logits match llama's to the same h_diff
  prediction (both engines share the identical dequant - the W rows match
  the reference dequant at corr 0.99996 vs llama's own outputs).
- GDN math: conv1d+SiLU, q/k norm, decay gate, sigmoid beta, delta rule all
  match llama.cpp's autoregressive path structurally and per-layer.
- Prefill chunking inside finchMoE: chunk 8/4/2/1 bit-identical, chunk 0
  within maxd 0.003.
- Tokenizer/decode: the SSE text assembly is correct (verified with
  FINCHMOE_SERVE_DEBUG token streams); the missing-newline tokens are model
  choices (e.g. `:result`), not dropped characters.

## The closing counterfactual (2026-08-27): NumPy offline forward

`scripts/numpy_forward.py` runs finchMoE's exact layer math — same GGUF
dequant (Q4_K/Q6_K, bit-identical vs the scalar reference), same GDN
delta-net / full-attention / MoE formulation, same norms, same final RMSNorm +
lm_head — but with numpy/BLAS-ordered matmuls. It was verified layer-by-layer
against finchMoE's own `FINCHMOE_LAYER_DUMP` at corr >= 0.999 (it reproduces
finchMoE's arithmetic faithfully; the achievable bar vs llama is
finchmoe-level ~0.985-0.999, with the L34 positions 118-135 spike being
inherent model sensitivity finchMoE also exhibits).

Then it was run as a generation engine on the identical evalplus prompts:

| engine | same 20 tasks (0-19) |
|---|---|
| llama.cpp CPU (`-ngl 0`), this Mac | 19/19 = 100% |
| **NumPy forward (finchMoE's math, BLAS order)** | **20/20 = 100%** |
| finchMoE Metal (identical GGUF, rep105) | 50% slice / 18.3% official 164 |

Deterministic: the slice run (5.3 h, resumable, no jetsam kill) reproduces the
standalone he0 smoke run character-for-character (675 chars, 1132s vs 1125s).
Full writeup: `scripts/NUMPY_VERDICT_2026-08-27.md`.

### What this closes

The identical math in BLAS order lands at 100% while the Metal build of the
same math lands at ~45-50% on the same slice. Therefore:

- formulation, dequant, GDN, FA, MoE, RoPE, norms, lm_head: ALL exonerated;
- the h_diff seed is the Metal-vs-CPU matmul reduction ordering (f32
  accumulator order + fast-math reassociation inside finchMoE's Metal
  kernels), which drifts the hidden state ~0.03 rms pre-norm at L38;
- the whole remaining gap 18.3% -> ~90% is that ordering difference.

### What this justifies (scope for the kernel work)

1. Instrument first: reuse the per-layer h_diff harness, swap one matmul family
   (delta-net / attention / MoE) at a time to a CPU/Accelerate fallback (the
   `--cpu-linear` pattern), measure h_diff rms + direction at L38. Rank the
   families; MoE is the usual suspect (largest reductions, 8 experts summed).
2. Cheap probe before surgery: randomize the reduction order (e.g. reorder
   expert summation) and check whether randomized drift already recovers the
   slice — would confirm the zero-mean reframing and might reveal a much
   smaller fix than full fixed-order accumulation.
3. Fix the dominant family with fixed-order fp32 accumulation (deterministic
   tree-order simdgroup sums, fp32 accumulators, no fast-math reassociation),
   re-run the 20-slice, iterate.
4. Stopping criterion: not h_diff = 0 — drift zero-mean enough that the slice
   lands ~90% (the llama band). Chasing bit-parity past that is wasted effort:
   llama's own configs disagree by more than that and all score 90%+.
5. Keep `--rep-penalty 1.05` shipped throughout; the kernel work lands as a
   separate, benchmarked change (rep105 fixed a real greedy attractor
   independent of the precision issue).

## Family-ranking instrumentation (2026-08-27): which matmul family seeds the h_diff?

Per the kernel-work plan, the three matmul families were swapped one at a time to
their CPU/Accelerate fallback on the exact eval config (GGUF + `--low-memory` +
`--prefill-chunk 0` + `--debug-layers`, 153-token sweep), each run producing a
full `FINCHMOE_LAYER_DUMP` (678 MB, position-major; chain-consistent:
`ou(L) == in(L+1)` exactly in every config). Reference: llama per-token l_out.

| config | what swapped | L38 rmsd vs llama (mean, 153 pos) | L38 h_diff direction cos vs baseline |
|---|---|---|---|
| baseline | (eval config as shipped) | **0.032764** | 1.0 |
| `--cpu-experts` | MoE experts GPU -> CPU | **0.032763** | 0.999930 |
| `FINCHMOE_GGUF_CPU_MATVEC=1` | projections GPU -> CPU | **0.032764** | 1.000000 |
| both | experts + projections -> CPU | **0.032764** | 1.000000 |
| `FINCHMOE_GGUF_GPU_ATTN=1` | FA attention CPU -> GPU | **0.032764** | 1.000000 |

### Result: L38 h-vs-llama is EXACTLY invariant (0.032764) across all 5 configs.

The h_diff direction does not move at all (cosine 0.99993+, i.e. the drift vector
is unchanged to 4+ decimals). Even the final greedy argmax is identical
(id 40) in all configs, final-logits maxd <= 0.003.

Digging into WHY the swaps don't help:

- **projections GPU -> CPU**: bit-identical (qkv maxd 5.7e-4) — the `matvec_qk`
  GPU kernel and `gguf_cpu_matvec` produce the same result. Exonerated.
- **experts GPU -> CPU**: real change (moe_out maxd 0.035, shared_out maxd 0.049
  in the `cm` tag) but **orthogonal** to the llama direction (corr(d_cfg, d_ll)
  = -0.087 at L38 pos 141; mean -0.01 across positions). It moves finchmoe
  sideways, not toward llama. Exonerated as the directional seed.
- **FA CPU -> GPU** (`FINCHMOE_GGUF_GPU_ATTN=1`): bit-identical (maxd 0.0).
  Exonerated.
- **GDN chain**: `can_gpu_linear` requires `!g_gguf_stage`, so the delta-net is
  ALREADY CPU in GGUF mode (`gpu_linear_attn=0` in the logs). This is why the
  earlier `--cpu-linear` probe was a no-op: it compared CPU-vs-CPU. The "GPU
  delta-net kernel is not the seed" conclusion was correct but for the wrong
  reason — the kernel was never active in the GGUF eval config.

### What this redirects

The eval config (`--gguf ... --low-memory`) is effectively an **all-CPU engine
plus GPU experts and GPU projections** — and both GPU families are exonerated.
The h_diff seed is therefore NOT in any Metal matmul kernel of the three named
families; it lives in the **always-CPU arithmetic** (GDN delta-net CPU chain, FA
attention CPU, and the CPU dequant matvec `gguf_cpu_matvec`) vs llama.cpp's CPU
arithmetic.

The NumPy counterfactual closes the loop: it reproduces finchmoe's per-layer
math at corr >= 0.999 (mean) while using BLAS-ordered matmuls, and scores 100%.
The 0.001-level per-layer differences between finchmoe's CPU accumulation order
and BLAS order are exactly the knife-edge that flips greedy whitespace decisions.
So the actionable lever is **finchmoe's CPU-side accumulation ordering (dequant
matvec / GDN / FA), not the Metal kernels** — equivalently, fixed-order fp32
accumulation matching llama/BLAS in the CPU paths the eval actually runs.

### Cheap-probe prediction

The user's optional probe (randomize reduction order / reorder expert
summation) is predicted NOT to recover the slice from this data: the expert
GPU->CPU difference is already a real reordering (maxd 0.02-0.05 at L38) and it
is orthogonal to the llama direction — randomizing it further stays orthogonal.
The promising probe is instead on the CPU dequant matvec / GDN / FA accumulation
order, matching BLAS.

### Correction: the old --cpu-linear conclusion was vacuous

The delta-net GPU kernel (`gated_delta_net_step`, dispatched in

exercised the CPU path. The new `FINCHMOE_CPU_DELTA=1` probe forces the real
CPU cblas branch and shows it too leaves h-vs-llama invariant.

### GDN internal check + the numpy-vs-finchmoe gap

numpy's GDN delta-out vs finchmoe's `de` tag at the same (pos, layer): corr

## BF16-norm-weight staging: hypothesized seed, then REFUTED at the L38 level (2026-08-28)

The GGUF importer stages F32 small tensors to BF16 by truncation
(`(uint16_t)(bits >> 16)`, infer.m `gguf_needs_bf16_stage`: norm.weight,
conv1d.weight, dt_bias, mlp.gate.weight, shared_expert_gate.weight,
in_proj_a/b.weight). The per-op norm test (identical input, token 0 layer 0)
confirmed finchmoe's `po` output == numpy's norm with BF16-truncated weights
to 0.003833 — the truncation is real and reproduces finchmoe exactly.

But the full-forward consequence was tested in numpy (RNE sim: exact vs
round-to-nearest-even vs truncation on all norm weights; L38 rmsd vs llama):

| norm weights | L38 p0 | L38 p50 | L38 p100 | L38 p135 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| exact F32 | 0.019894 | 0.020160 | 0.034170 | 0.218216 |
| RNE BF16 | 0.019945 | 0.020921 | 0.035097 | 0.221088 |
| trunc BF16 (finchmoe) | 0.019695 | 0.019940 | 0.029366 | 0.217395 |

**Norm-weight precision does NOT move L38** (exact vs trunc differ by
<0.005, and at p0/p50 trunc is actually CLOSER to llama). The BF16 norm
truncation is a real per-op difference but is NOT the L38 h_diff driver.
`output_norm` truncation was also exonerated at the logits level: it flips
0/153 greedy top-1 tokens on the sweep.

### Slice-level counterfactual (decisive)

**RESULT (2026-08-28, complete): numpy with ALL staged ops BF16-truncated
(mirroring finchmoe's GGUF importer exactly — norms, conv1d, dt_bias,
routers, alpha/beta) scores 20/20 = 100% on the eval-config slice.**

generation-level seed — the failure lives in finchmoe's arithmetic (the
~0.001-level-per-layer distributed difference in its always-CPU paths), not
in its weights and not in any single swappable op (11-probe sweep above).

Combined verdict of the 2026-08-28 session:

1. Norm-weight BF16 truncation is real (per-op 0.003833, reproduces

2. All 11 single-op/order hypotheses are refuted at L38: norm weights,
   output_norm, delta-net GPU-vs-cblas, GDN chain (de matches 0.0006 at

3. The divergence is position-carrying (p0 0.003 → p135 0.18) and grows
   with depth; the GDN chain CONTRACTS input differences while the
   residual/MoE stream carries them. It is a distributed arithmetic
   difference in finchmoe's always-CPU implementation, not a localized
   weight or ordering issue — consistent with the family-ranking and
   step-3-probe conclusions. No single-op "fix the localized op" target
   exists in this config; the path to the llama band is matching
   finchmoe's CPU arithmetic to numpy/BLAS more closely across the board
   (or a data-driven logit correction), not a weight-staging change.

### Step-1 probe (2026-08-28): expert-path bit-parity — EXONERATED

The last unverified execution path — the routed-expert dequant + matvec +
SwiGLU + weighted combine (8 active experts × 3 GEMMs per layer, the largest
per-layer numerical payload) — was compared bit-for-bit:

- **GPU fused Q4_K kernel vs CPU `gguf_cpu_matvec`**: moe_out identical to
